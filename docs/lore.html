The add-on is a story-driven adventure. The journal you receive when you first join gives you one clue at a time; each clue sends you to a real place, a real fight and a real reward. This document is the long version.

1. Before the Eclipse

Long ago the world had one sky and one dawn that never ended. There was no Overworld, no Nether, no End - only the Dawnlands, a single bright country under a single patient sun. The First Dawn was not a star. It was the world's heartbeat: a light that remembered every living thing and made room for all of it.

Where that light fell hardest it hardened into crystal. People later called the crystal Azurite and thought it was only a pretty stone. It is the First Dawn, frozen where it fell.

2. The Hollow Sun

Nobody knows what the Eclipse was before it rose. The old songs call it the Hollow Sun: a second sun that gave no light, only a hunger for the first one. It rose over the Dawnlands and swallowed the First Dawn whole.

The world cracked.

  • What stayed in the daylight became the Overworld.
  • What fell into the Eclipse's heat became the Burning Deep (the Nether).
  • What was thrown past the edge of the sky became the Void Shore (the End).

The Eclipse's power shattered with it - into Seven Shards. A Shard is a piece of the Hollow Sun: it wants to be whole again, and it whispers to anything that holds it. Two of the world's strange new materials are what the Shards left behind:

MaterialWhat it is
AzuriteThe First Dawn, frozen into crystal.
PyreliteThe Eclipse's blood, cooled in the Burning Deep.
EclipsiteThe Eclipse's shadow, turned to stone on the Void Shore.

Carry all three and the Eclipse notices.

3. The Seven Seals and the Seven Keepers

Seven people refused to let the Shards be reunited. Each took one Shard and bound it into a Seal - a prison and a lock in one - and swore to guard it: the Seven Keepers.

A Seal is dangerous to keep. Break one carelessly and the Shard bleeds into its Keeper. The longer a Keeper holds a Seal alone, the more the Shard speaks with the Keeper's own voice. This is the central tragedy of the story: the guardians are slowly becoming the thing they guard.

4. The Hollow Court and the Hollow Lords

The Eclipse does not only hide. It collects.

Anything that dies near a Shard can be taught to serve a sun that gives no light. These are the Hollow Court:

  • Gravewalkers - the buried, dragged back up in their burial shrouds.
  • Revenants - soldiers who never got to stop fighting.
  • Cave Weavers - the Silkqueen's unfinished lullabies, spun into spiders.
  • Sporelings - the seeds a dying forest could no longer plant.

Above them stand three Hollow Lords. They are not Keepers and they hold no Seal. They are casualties: great beings the Eclipse broke and kept.

  • The Hollow King - a mortal king who bargained with the Eclipse to cheat death. It kept its word: he cannot die, and he cannot rest. His court wanders the graveyards serving a throne that no longer exists. His relic, the Grave Sigil, was once a key to a Keeper's shrine.
  • The Mosswood Colossus - the forest's last Greenwarden. When the Eclipse withered the sun its roots drank shadow instead, and the guardian woke wrong. Its relic is the Verdant Heart.
  • The Silkqueen - the Mother of Threads, who spun webs to hold the cracking world together until the Eclipse taught her that everything caught is hers. Her relic is the Silk Idol.

The Hollow Lords are side stories: fight them for loot, relics and lore pages, but the road of the Seals does not pass through them.

5. The Wayfarer

Someone walked this road before you.

Her name has been scratched out of every page. She was a traveller who decided the Eclipse could be ended by finding every Seal, and she kept a journal as she went. The journal in your pack is hers. Its pages fill only as you walk where she walked - and the ink gets shorter after the second Seal. Her last legible line is about the Second Keeper: "He knew my name."

What happened to her is the oldest unanswered question in the story.

6. Chapter I - The Veiled Blossom

"Where pink snow never melts and the trees never stop blooming, the First Keeper sleeps. Walk beneath the blossoms. Listen."

Hanae was the miko - the shrine maiden - of the Cherry Grove. She was the first Keeper to take a Shard, and she bound it with her own life: she wove a veil of petals over her face so that the Shard could not look out through her eyes, and she has kept it on ever since. She sees the Eclipse in every mirror.

She never wanted to be a boss. Over the years, travellers came to the grove - pilgrims, adventurers, people who wanted the Seal. Each time she tried to make them turn back. Each time they kept coming. The Veiled Echoes that follow her are the reflections of those she could not turn around.

Her message is the same for everyone who finds her: the end of this road is impossible. Stop.

The fight

She does not fight to kill. Her three phases are three ways of telling you to leave:

1. The Veil. She fans petal blades at you, steps behind you through the mirror, and splits into Veiled Echoes - reflections that shield her until you shatter them. 2. Falling Blossoms. The fog turns pink and thick. Petals fall in rings you can see coming; Blossom Vexes - little masked spirits of her grief - harry you. 3. The Eclipse Bloom. The Shard starts to speak through her. Her colours darken, the fog turns black-red, and the petals spiral outwards. She begs you to run while she can still stay her hand.

If you are badly hurt she lowers her parasol and stops until you recover. She would rather lose than kill.

"Turn back, wayfarer. I do not wish to hurt you."
"Seven Seals... and the Eclipse waits behind the last."
"No one who walked this road has ever returned."
"I was like you once. I read the same journal."
"The ending was never meant to be reached."

When she falls, she gives up the Seal anyway:

"...You will not stop, will you? Then take my Seal, wayfarer. And please... do not let the Eclipse take you too."
"...thank you. Six Seals remain."

Rewards: the Veilbloom Regalia (a full set of pink armour as strong as netherite, with a Speed and Jump Boost set bonus), Veilbloom Shards (to repair it), the Seal of the Blossom, diamonds, an enchanted golden apple and a heap of Pink Petals.

7. Chapter II - The Second Seal

The journal's next entry is short and frightened:

"The First Seal is ours... but her plea will not leave me. The Second Keeper stands at the edge of the world - the Eclipse Sovereign, in the End. He knew my name when I found him."

The Eclipse Sovereign was once the Keeper of the Void Shore. He stood at the edge of the world and watched the Eclipse rise. When the Shards scattered he did something none of the other Keepers dared: he took the Second Shard into himself to spare everyone else from it. It worked. It also made him the Eclipse's herald.

His Voidweavers are his unspoken doubts, given robes and spells. The Eclipse Core he leaves behind when he falls is the Shard's husk.

Defeating him completes Chapter II. How the Sovereign came to know the Wayfarer's name - and why the Wayfarer's journal gets thinner after him - is where the story is waiting.

9. What happens next

The story is deliberately unfinished. The next Seals are not yet written. Things the journal already hints at, which later updates will explore:

  • Why the Wayfarer's notes end after the Second Seal.
  • What the Seven Shards become if they are ever reunited.
  • Whether a Keeper can be freed from a Seal without a death.
  • Who the Eclipse was, before it was hungry.

10. For writers (how the lore is built so it can grow)

  • Seals and Keepers are the main line. Each Seal gets a Keeper, a place, a fight with a reason (not just an obstacle), and a final plea or secret.
  • Side bosses are the Hollow Lords: casualties of the Eclipse that give loot, relics and lore, never the main clue.
  • The journal is the only narrator. Every clue is a Wayfarer quote followed by a plain instruction in a second colour.
  • Each chapter is data. Adding a chapter means adding steps and lore pages in tools/scripts/story.js (see CLAUDE.md).
  • Keepers are tragedies, not villains. The Eclipse is the villain; everyone else is paying for it.
